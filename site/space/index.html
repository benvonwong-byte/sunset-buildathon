How this works

Where the data comes from

  • Storefronts and hours: OpenStreetMap (Oct 3, 2026). Where it has no hours, they were read from the business's own website when it posts them clearly.
  • Registrations: DataSF Registered Business Locations (Oct 9, 2026) confirm a place is active. Places that appear only in the city registry are left off, since a registration can name a person at a home address.
  • Building size, stories, use: SF Assessor property roll, 2025. Lots and zoning: DataSF parcels. Footprints and streets: OpenStreetMap.

How square footage is estimated

Each business is matched to its lot. The Assessor's building area is divided by the number of stories to get the ground floor, checked against the mapped footprint, cut 15% for walls and stairs, then split among the businesses on that lot. Good means the two sources agree and at most two businesses share the floor. Rough means something didn't line up. Treat every number as a starting point for a conversation with the owner.

Limits

  • National and large regional chains, banks and gas stations are left out on purpose.
  • Most storefronts don't have posted hours in open data yet. They show under "No hours" and aren't counted in the totals.
  • Hours describe a typical week. Holidays, seasonal hours and recent changes aren't reflected.
  • "Closed" means closed to customers. Staff may still be inside.
  • The drawings show the lot (dashed) and building footprint from city and map data. Street View photos come from Google.
